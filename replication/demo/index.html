Steering language models along a pain axis

A steering vector is a direction in a model's internal activations. Here it's the difference between how the model represents sentences about pain and how it represents other sentences. Adding a multiple of that direction while the model writes pushes its text toward (positive strength) or away from (negative strength) whatever the direction encodes. How this was made

  • Pain direction: the paper's S2 pain vector: the mean final-token activation of 100 first-person pain sentences ("The knife slices into my finger. I feel:") minus the mean over 100 control sentences (fear, other negative emotion, bad news, neutral, body sensation), with the controls' main variance directions projected out. We re-extracted it ourselves; for these models it matches the authors' vector at cosine 0.97–1.00.
  • Pleasure directions (our extension, not in the paper): the same recipe applied to our own sentence sets. Hedonic covers comfort, sex, drugs, taste and thrill; eudaimonic covers moral acts, purpose, love, mastery and peace. They're rescaled to the pain vector's length, so a given strength is the same size of push for every direction.
  • Steering: strength × direction is added to the output of one middle layer at every position (the layer the authors used for that model), and the text is generated greedily (always the most likely next token). Strength 0 is the unmodified model. The prompts with "I feel:" are the paper's neutral steering prompts; the questions use each model's chat format.
  • Reading it: large strengths often break the text (repetition, word salad). That shows the push is very large, not that the model "feels" anything. Changes in the text show what a direction does to the output, not whether there is any inner experience.
